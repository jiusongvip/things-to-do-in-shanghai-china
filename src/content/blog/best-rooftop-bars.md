Shanghai has one of the world's great skylines, and its rooftop bars are built to show it off. But not every rooftop is worth the premium you'll pay for the view. Some are overpriced tourist traps; others are quiet, local, and genuinely excellent. This is the honest, ranked shortlist for 2026, judged on drink quality, view, vibe, and value.

Before the list, one framing idea that will save you money: in Shanghai, **you are usually paying for the view, not the liquid**. The best strategy is often to order one light, cold drink and let the skyline do the work — rather than committing to a full bottle list you'll never finish.

## The top five, ranked

### 1. Flair Rooftop, Ritz-Carlton Pudong

Still the reference point for a reason. On the 52nd floor of the Shanghai Tower cluster (yes, the tower-shaped needle on the east of the skyline), Flair has a circular terrace with a 360-degree view that takes in **both** the Pudong towers and the French Concession across the river.

- **The view**: unbeatable. You see both skylines and the river bend at once.
- **The cost**: expect ¥100+ for a cocktail; a reservation is strongly recommended.
- **Order**: their signature lychee spritz; skip the overpriced champagne lists.
- **Best time**: sunset, but arrive or book well in advance for an edge seat.

### 2. The Bellagio (尚合) Rooftop, West Bund

The impressive new riverside contender. A huge open terrace along the West Bund, a good food menu, and a view pointed straight at the Pudong skyline in the distance. More room than Flair and a younger, less corporate crowd — which many prefer.

### 3. Sir Elly's, The Peninsula

Classic and understated, on the Bund side looking back at Pudong. Quieter than the rooftop giants, with a genuinely refined cocktail list. The trade-off: the view is a touch further away because it's lower than the Pudong towers.

## The honest advice on managing the night

- **Don't pay for a table "holding fee"** at the trendiest spots. There's usually a free-bar area and rail-side standing; just ask.
- **Book the late slot** (after 9 PM) for the fully lit skyline — the kitchen closes early, so eat beforehand.
- **Check the dress code** at hotel rooftops (Flair, the Peninsula): no flip-flops, mostly smart casual.
- **Weekends are intense.** For a calm, un-hassled visit, go on a weekday.

## The underrated locals' picks

- **Vue Bar, Hyatt on the Bund** — slightly better value than the big names, strong service, and a terrace over the river bend.
- **Planet One** — genuine dive-bar energy with a sofa terrace; not fancy, but the view still does all the work.
- **The Long Bar & Terrace** (West Bund) — cheap for the skyline it gives, and a favorite of locals.

## Why the skyline view matters

The great thing about a rooftop isn't just the romance — it's comprehension. Standing above the river, you finally grasp how Shanghai is organized: the old Puxi bank of colonial-era buildings and retail streets to the west, and the glassy Pudong financial cluster to the east, all connected by the river and its bridges. The skyline makes the city's geography legible in a way no map does.

## Booking, timing and budget — the practical details

Rooftop bars in Shanghai are not cheap, and the difference between an okay night and a great one often has little to do with the drink menu. Get these right and you'll enjoy it far more.

**When to book.** For a sunset or weekend slot at Flair, Sir Elly's, or Vue, reserve at least **3–5 days ahead** — weekend edge-tables go fast. The Bellagio's larger terrace is easier to walk into. On weekdays you can often just show up before 7 PM.

**Timing for the light.** Golden hour hits roughly **30 minutes before sunset**. If you want the skyline transition (pink light → city lights), arrive about 30–40 minutes before sunset and stay through the switch at around sunset. If you go after 9 PM, everything is fully lit and it simply looks like the Shanghai in every photo.

**Watch your spend.** A "holding fee" or a minimum-spend on a full bottle is the biggest trap. Instead: order one or two drinks from the bar or at the rail, settle up, and leave happy. A couple of cocktails is the sensible spend for an hour of skyline — not a bottle of Champagne you'll never finish.

**What to skip.** Any place advertising a "couples' package" or a minimum spend to sit indoors is trading money for a view you won't actually get. Always push for outside terrace seating.

## Frequently asked about rooftop bars in Shanghai

**What's the cheapest way to see the skyline at night?** Apart from the free Bund promenade, the public ferry (Pudong ↔ Puxi) at night costs about ¥2 and gives you a riverside-and-skyline view from the water. Rooftop bars are for the *drink + experience* — choose them deliberately.

**Is a reservation really needed?** At the top hotel rooftops, yes, for sunset and weekends. Weeknights and the more standing-friendly terraces (Planet One, The Long Bar) are fine to drop into.

**Can I have a non-alcoholic drink?** Yes, most bars serve fresh juice or iced tea against the skyline; only the strictest hotel spots insist on a minimum order.

**Which bar is best on a tight budget?** Planet One (mostly the ¥50–70 range) or The Long Bar for the same skyline at a fraction of the hotel prices.

## Parting tip

Use your rooftop visit as an evening anchor, not a full stop. End dinner, catch a golden-hour drink for the skyline transition, then move on — either to a riverside walk or a late-night lane. Two or three drinks across two venues beats hours at one.
