Shanghai rewards photographers who get up early — and photography punishes those who don't. The city has two skylines, miles of back lanes, and some of the finest Art Deco in Asia, all of which photograph dramatically. But the difference between a good shot and a great one in Shanghai is almost always **light and timing**, not gear.

This guide is the honest, ordered list: the ten locations that deliver, the exact best time for each, the lens that helps, and the one simple habit that will improve every frame you take.

## The two rules that beat everything

1. **Shoot within two hours of sunrise or sunset.** Shanghai's flat, humid light at midday erases depth and flatten the skyline. Long light is gold.
2. **Go early for crowds and clean frames.** At sunrise the Bund is near-empty; by 9 AM it's shoulder-to-shoulder.

## 1. The Bund at sunrise

Non-negotiable first stop. Arrive on the east side of the Bund promenade (the rail with the wide view back to Pudong) before first light. The sun rises **behind** Pudong, silhouetting the tower cluster and the Oriental Pearl against a warm band.

- **Time**: 40 minutes before sunrise; light and no crowds.
- **Lens**: 16–35mm for the sweep.

## 2. The Bund at blue hour (from the opposite bank)

After sunset, cross to the Pudong side (Line 2 to Lujiazui) and shoot back toward the colonnaded colonial Bund. The new railing along the Pudong towers gives a clean, elevated line over the water.

- **Time**: 15–40 minutes after sunset — the transition dusk.
- **Lens**: 24–70mm.

## 3. The Shanghai Tower from the ground

On a clear day the tower's twisted glass facade is a stunning subject from the plaza below. Get low and shoot up for the full 632-metre bend.

- **Time**: clear morning, 8–10 AM for clean light without harsh glare.

## 4. The French Concession lanes (Wukang Road)

The iconic statement: plane-tree-canopied lanes, old apartment buildings, and bicycles. Shoot at golden hour for the dappled canopy light.

- **Time**: weekday late afternoon for quieter lanes and low sun.
- **Lens**: 50mm; the compression flatters the street.

## 5. Yu Garden rooftops (from the Nine-Bend Bridge)

From the Nine-Bend Bridge above the koi pond, frame the upturned eaves and carved roof ridges of the Old City rooftops. The detail work here rewards a telephoto or macro lens, as the tilework and glazing are the real subject.

- **Time**: early morning, before the crowds and harsh contrast.

## 6. The Huangpu River ferry

A ¥2 public ferry from Puxi to Pudong (departs from the Bund). From the rail you can shoot straight along the river, the two skylines cleaving the frame with distance.

- **Time**: golden hour either direction, but avoid the rush.

## 7. The Art Deco walk (off the Bund)

The hidden Deco facades shine on any weather window — cloud-light softens the stonework. Walk Jing'an and the ornate 1930s department stores and former banks along Nanjing West Road.

- **Time**: overcast morning for even light on the stone.

## 8. Night light trails (beneath the Yan'an Elevated)

Stand under the Yan'an Elevated road for long-exposure streaks of red taillights over the asphalt. The classic Shanghai night shot.

- **Gear**: tripod is mandatory; a neutral-density filter helps.
- **Time**: 9 PM onward, weeknights for less traffic congestion in frame.

## 9. Century Park lake reflections

Wide mirror-smooth reflections of the tower clusters across the lakes at dusk. Long exposure with a small aperture pulls the water to silk.

- **Time**: sunset, calm evenings.
- **Gear**: tripod.

## 10. Any elevated platform at dawn

Close the portfolio with a morning panorama: a hotel rooftop or an elevated Metro crosswalk where the rising sun floods the skyline. It's the last shot that stitches the set together.

- **Time**: dawn, brightest 10 minutes after sunrise.

## When each season gives you

Shanghai's light varies more with the seasons than most cities:

- **Spring (late Mar–May)** — the classic photography window: clear air, blossoms, and long tidy evenings. Best overall.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug)** — the skyline is stunning at dusk but the haze can be brutal until submerged storms clear it. Sunsets are late (after 7 PM); be flexible about when it rains.
- **Autumn (Oct–Nov)** — crisp blue skies and golden evenings; arguably the cleanest air of the year for skyline shots.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb)** — shortest days, sharp morning light, and the emptiest pancake hour.

## A sample half-day photo crawl

If you have one morning and want a complete set, follow this rhythm:

- **5:40 AM** — Bund sunrise (No. 1). Back against the railing, wide lens, expect the light to go gold fast.
- **7:30 AM** — Breakfast (a corner jianbing), then the French Concession lanes (No. 4) before the population wakes.
- **9:30 AM** — Art Deco walk (No. 7) in the softer mid-morning light.
- **Sunset (evening)** — ferry crossing (No. 6) for the two-skylines frame, then Century Park (No. 9) if you want reflections, or the Yan'an trails (No. 8) after dark.

That sequence gives you a sunrise, a portrait-walk, an architectural set, and a night light shot in a single trip — a proper mini-portfolio by the time you're done.

## The shots that aren't worth the effort

Not every "famous" photo op is worth your time. Honest list:

- **The street shot of the Pearl Tower from the surrounding plaza** is rarely better than the elevated views — the colours are fine, but it's a tourist-selfie corner that eats time for a picture you can get better elsewhere.
- **The Bund "colonnade dead-centre" postcard** only works from one specific unblocked viewpoint; the shot everyone wants rarely turns out as sharp as the adverts suggest without controlled conditions.
- **Rooftop drone shots** — don't bother: the drone ban means you'll never legally get the air shots you see online.

## Choosing gear by how you shoot

- **One variable lens (24–70mm) covers 80% of the city** — pack light, walk far.
- **A small tripod** is essential for the night trails and reflections.
- **Leave the drone at home**: drone flying is restricted across central Shanghai and the authorities enforce it. Stick to elevated platforms instead.
- **A circular polarizer** helps slice glare off glass and the river on sunny days.

## The one habit that changes your whole trip

Check the weather in the morning and **shift your day to chase the golden hour**. If it's overcast midday, that's perfect for the Deco walk; if it clears overnight, your Bund sunrise is on. Shanghai's look changes with the weather, and the flexible photographer gets the city at its best.
